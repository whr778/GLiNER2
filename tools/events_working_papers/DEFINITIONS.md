A **dose-response curve** is a graphical representation that plots the magnitude of a biological response against the dose or concentration of a stimulus, typically resulting in a **sigmoidal (S-shaped)** curve. The **x-axis** usually represents the logarithm of the dose or concentration, while the **y-axis** represents the response, often expressed as a percentage of the maximal effect.

Key features include:
*   **Threshold**: The lowest dose at which a measurable response occurs.
*   **Potency**: Indicated by the **EC50** (half-maximal effective concentration), which is the dose required to produce 50% of the maximal response; a lower EC50 indicates higher potency.
*   **Efficacy**: The maximal response (**Emax**) a drug can achieve, regardless of dose.
*   **Slope**: Reflects the change in response per unit dose increase, with steeper slopes indicating higher potency.

These curves are essential in pharmacology and toxicology for determining **drug efficacy**, **safety margins**, and **therapeutic indices**, helping to establish safe dosage guidelines and understand mechanisms of action.
